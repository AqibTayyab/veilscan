## 2. What VeilScan Is

VeilScan is a **network security audit tool** that scans IP addresses and hostnames to discover open network ports, identify running services, detect software versions, and generate security reports.

VeilScan is designed for:
- Security education and learning
- Authorized network audits of systems you own
- Authorized penetration testing engagements
- Home lab environments (e.g., Metasploitable, VulnHub)
- CTF (Capture The Flag) competitions

## 3. Authorized Use Only — The Most Important Section

### 3.1 You Must Have Permission

**You may ONLY use VeilScan to scan systems that:**
- You own outright (your own computers, servers, devices)
- You operate and control (your company's network infrastructure)
- You have received **explicit written permission** to scan from the system owner

**The following are authorized targets:**
- Your own machines (192.168.x.x home network, VMs, lab machines)
- Systems in a CTF challenge where scanning is explicitly permitted
- Dedicated practice environments (Metasploitable, DVWA, HackTheBox, TryHackMe)
- `scanme.nmap.org` — explicitly authorized by the Nmap Project for scanning

### 3.2 Unauthorized Scanning Is Illegal

Port scanning without permission is illegal in many jurisdictions, including but not limited to:

| Country | Relevant Law |
|---------|-------------|
| United States | Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA), 18 U.S.C. § 1030 |
| United Kingdom | Computer Misuse Act 1990 |
| European Union | Directive 2013/40/EU on attacks against information systems |
| Germany | StGB §202a, §303a, §303b |
| Australia | Criminal Code Act 1995, Part 10.7 |

**You acknowledge that:**
- Port scanning a system without authorization may constitute unauthorized access
- Even if no data is accessed, the scan itself may be illegal
- You are solely responsible for ensuring your scans are authorized
- Aqib Tayyab and VeilScan contributors bear no liability for unauthorized use

### 3.3 Consent Prompt

VeilScan displays a legal consent prompt before scanning public IP addresses. By answering "yes" or using `--agree`, you confirm that you have authorization to scan the target. Providing false confirmation does not transfer liability to VeilScan or its author.
